Week 5 has put several high-profile NFL offenses in uncertain positions. Philadelphia running back Saquon Barkley and Baltimore quarterback Lamar Jackson were listed as non-participants in practice, while Washington quarterback Jayden Daniels returned as a full participant. Those three cases point in different directions, but none should be reduced to a prediction made from one practice report.

The useful question is what each update changes for the team around the player. Official participation reports establish what happened in practice. Team reporting adds context. Final game designations and inactive lists provide the clearest answers closer to kickoff.

## Barkley's absence changes Philadelphia's preparation

The [NFL's Week 5 injury report](https://www.nfl.com/injuries/) listed Barkley as a non-participant. [FOX Sports reported](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nfl/nfl-catchup-eagles-injuries-mount-jayden-daniels-trending-toward-return) that Barkley and receiver DeVonta Smith missed Wednesday practice with hamstring injuries as Philadelphia prepared for its London game against Jacksonville. The report described Barkley as week-to-week and said the injury was not believed to be long term.

That leaves Philadelphia with two separate decisions. The medical staff must judge Barkley's readiness, while the coaching staff must prepare the running game without assuming he will be available. A running back's absence can affect pass protection, short-yardage calls and the distribution of early-down touches. It can also place more pressure on an offense already managing other injuries.

The practical signal is participation over the rest of the week. A move from no practice to limited work would provide more information than speculation about a Sunday return. Until the Eagles issue a final designation, readers should treat his availability as unresolved.

## Baltimore must prepare two versions of its offense

Jackson's status carries a different tactical consequence because Baltimore's offense is built around his passing, movement and decision-making. The [Ravens said Jackson did not practice Wednesday](https://www.baltimoreravens.com/news/lamar-jackson-not-at-first-practice-following-ankle-injury-ravens-falcons-week-5) after leaving the second half of Baltimore's 24-18 win over Tennessee. Head coach Jesse Minter said the team would evaluate him through the week and did not rule out a player of Jackson's experience competing without a normal practice schedule.

That possibility does not remove the need for a backup plan. Tyler Huntley replaced Jackson against Tennessee and took practice work while Jackson received treatment. If Jackson cannot play, Baltimore loses more than a starting quarterback. The shape of designed runs, option concepts and off-schedule passing changes too.

For Atlanta, the uncertainty complicates defensive preparation. A plan built to contain Jackson's mobility is not identical to one designed for Huntley. Baltimore can preserve some ambiguity until the final designation, but the team also has to decide how much practice time each quarterback needs.

## Daniels offers a more encouraging comparison

Washington received a different signal. The official report listed Daniels as a full participant after his elbow injury. FOX Sports reported that he practiced with a brace and was moving toward a possible return against the Giants.

Full participation is stronger evidence of readiness than sideline work or a limited session, but it is still one step in the process. Washington must see how Daniels responds after practice and decide whether the brace affects any part of the game plan. The final designation remains the team's formal statement about his availability.

The contrast with Barkley and Jackson is useful. All three are major offensive players, yet the same word — injury — covers very different stages of recovery. Daniels completed the team's session. Barkley and Jackson did not participate. Their teams therefore face different levels of uncertainty entering the final preparation days.

## What to check before kickoff

Readers following these situations should use a simple order of evidence:

1. Check the official practice report and whether participation is full, limited or absent.
2. Read the team's final game designation rather than relying on an early-week forecast.
3. Check the inactive list released before kickoff.
4. Separate a player's availability from assumptions about workload. Active does not always mean a normal role.

That approach is less dramatic than reacting to every update, but it is more accurate. Week 5 will test roster depth in Philadelphia and Baltimore while offering Washington a possible route back toward its preferred offense. The most reliable answers will come from the final reports, not from the loudest prediction.
